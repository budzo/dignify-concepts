LEADERSHIP & CULTURE

Is accountability
killing your culture—
or fueling it?

A different perspective on accountability starts with understanding the person behind the performance.

Joe KiedingerCEO, Dignify
October 22, 2025Adapted article preview
Read the original ↗

Talk about your team ↗

Accountability can feel like a threat. It can also be an invitation to do work that brings out someone’s best. The difference begins with how well a leader understands the person.

Start with ability.

In the original article, Joe Kiedinger asks leaders to consider accountability through the lens of ability: a person’s natural strengths and the skills they have developed. The goal shifts toward finding a better fit between the individual and the work.

Clear expectations still matter. But expectations alone cannot resolve a mismatch between someone’s strengths and their responsibilities.

Look beyond the outcome.

A role that energizes one person may exhaust another. Before treating a struggle as a lack of effort, consider the person’s motivations, experience, and readiness for the task.

Could this be a question of alignment?

A reflection inspired by the article

Make room for a conversation.

The next step is an honest, compassionate discussion. Explore whether the person has the abilities and support the work requires. Understanding creates a more useful starting point than assumptions.

PUT IT INTO PRACTICE

One person.
One better conversation.

Choose someone whose responsibilities may need a fresh look. Use these prompts to open a discussion:

  • Which parts of your work give you energy?
  • Where would more clarity or support help?
  • What could we adjust together?
